This month I had a gap between projects with nothing to do. Seeing how gorgeous the fall foliage in Western Sichuan looked, I set off alone from Chengdu on a self-drive trip along the Lixiao Road, doing the whole thing in a single day. The route ran clockwise — Chengdu to Xiaojin, then on to Lixian before looping back to Chengdu, over 500 kilometers in all. The highland mountain roads are rugged and the lanes narrow, so my average speed was only 50–60 km/h; just the driving ate up around eight hours, leaving the sightseeing time a bit tight.

Traveling solo is wonderfully free — you go wherever you please. I skipped every paid scenic area. When I got hungry, I'd pull over by the roadside, start a campfire, boil up some noodles and brew a pot of tea. Aside from about 100 yuan in tolls and a little over 200 yuan in gas, I didn't spend a single extra cent.

With a long-range EV charging at home for 0.28 yuan per kilowatt-hour, the energy cost could be even lower. Once you climb to the highest point along the route — the Lixiao Tunnel at 3,900 meters — the remaining 240 kilometers back to Chengdu can be covered on 20 kilowatt-hours or less. A gift from nature: the farther you drive, the more charge you have.

Balang Mountain. This is the first high point. Many years ago, when there was no tunnel yet, you had to cross over the Balang Mountain pass at around 4,500 meters. Snow lined both sides of the road and the bitter wind howled — brutally cold. Now that there's a tunnel, it's so much easier.

Red Stone Beach. At first I thought the rocks were red because of high iron content. Later I learned it's actually a type of algae that only grows at high altitudes, coating the rocks — its outer layer is rich in astaxanthin, which helps protect against the fierce ultraviolet radiation up here.
